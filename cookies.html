1. Change your choice

Whatever you picked, you can pick again. Nothing is remembered longer than twelve months — after that we ask you afresh.

2. What a cookie is

A cookie is a small file a website asks your browser to keep, so that something can be remembered between one page and the next. Some are essential — without them a shopping basket would empty every time you clicked. Others exist to count visitors, or to let an advertiser recognise you on another site later.

The rules in the UK (PECR, alongside UK GDPR) say the essential ones are fine, and everything else needs your permission first. That is why you saw a banner before anything measured or advertised.

This page also covers local storage, which does the same job by a different mechanism. We've listed those too rather than hide behind the word “cookie”.

5. Not cookies, but worth knowing

  • Google Fonts. The typefaces are served by Google, so Google receives your IP address when a page loads. This happens before any cookie choice because it's part of drawing the page. No cookie is set by it.
  • The booking diary. “Book a 30-minute call” opens a diary run by LeadConnector (HighLevel) on their own domain, under their own cookie rules. Nothing is set here until you go there.
  • The forms and the assistant. What you type goes to our CRM. That's not a cookie — it's you deliberately telling us something — and it's covered in the privacy policy.
  • Server logs. Our host records requests, including IP addresses, to keep the site up and defend it. No cookie involved, and we can't switch it off any more than you can stop a postman seeing an address.

6. How consent works here

  • Off by default. Measurement and advertising start denied. The tags aren't loaded, so nothing can quietly fire while you decide.
  • Refusing is as easy as accepting. “Reject all” sits next to “Accept all” in the same size button. It is not hidden behind a second screen.
  • Google Consent Mode v2. If you accept advertising, your consent travels with the Google tag. If you don't, Google receives no advertising identifiers from you at all.
  • Global Privacy Control. If your browser sends a GPC signal, we treat it as a refusal and don't show the banner. You can still turn things on deliberately.
  • Withdrawing is one click, from the button in section 1 or the “Cookie settings” link in the footer of every page. Withdrawing stops any further collection; it can't undo what was collected while consent was in place.
